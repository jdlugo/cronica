# First-time arcade study kit

Engineering is ready for a small qualitative study; no participants have been recruited or tested in this session.

Recruit 6–8 people unfamiliar with the app across low, moderate and high movie knowledge. Record device, app build, prior app familiarity and movie knowledge. Participation compensation, if any, must not depend on rounds completed or returning tomorrow.

Opening instruction: “Explore this app as you normally would. You can stop whenever you want.” Do not mention Arcade until independent discovery is observed. Record Home exposure, navigation, interpretation of the recommended entry, first accepted action, first earned success, help/skip, confusion and independently chosen replay. Ask neutral expectations after observing behavior; do not reveal answers or coach.

Allow unprompted stopping. Afterward ask what felt satisfying, what felt like work, what seemed repeated, and what they would choose again. Separate familiarity with a movie/actor from reasoning about the mechanic. Preference ratings supplement behavior; they are not retention.

Invite an optional next-day session without requiring attendance or offering a return-specific reward. Record actual return and the stated reason, plus whether tomorrow's content feels new. Do not interpret this small recruited cohort as organic D1 retention or a powered experiment.

Show the existing Remove Ads page after gameplay and ask what the purchase changes and whether they expect payment is needed to finish. Verify comprehension of an optional app-wide ad-free upgrade before testing an offer after success.

Decision gate: prioritize reproduced discoverability/control/score blockers. Use observed reasons to refine the featured entry and content; establish exposure baselines before selecting a quantitative sample size/MDE. Keep an exit/skip count and exact eligible participant denominator rather than reporting only the finishers.
